# Shot Grammar

Vocabulary with consequences. Every entry answers: what does this choice let the audience read, and what does it cost?

## 1. Shot size

| Size | Frame | Audience reads | Cost / risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| EWS / extreme wide | subject is small in a large world | scale, isolation, geography, "where are we" | no face, no performance; needs strong value design |
| WS / wide | full body plus environment | body language, spatial relations, movement | emotional distance; details vanish |
| FS / full | head to feet, context visible | gesture, posture, blocking | the most abandoned size in modern film — it is the size that lets bodies act |
| MLS / medium long | knees or waist up | gesture plus situation | transitional; rarely a destination |
| MS / medium | waist up | gesture and readable face; group relations | the workhorse of dialogue, not the emotional peak |
| MCU / medium close | chest up | expression plus a little context | the default for speech |
| CU / close | head | reaction, thought, micro-performance | the cause of the reaction leaves the frame — you owe it elsewhere |
| ECU / extreme close | eye, mouth, hands, object | intimacy, obsession, threat, detail as event | one shot only; repeats feel like a tic |
| Two-shot | two subjects, equal weight | relationship, balance, distance between them | both must be doing something |
| OTS / over-the-shoulder | one back + one face | conversation, dominance by line of sight | the shoulder silently assigns power |

Rules that follow from the sizes:

- **A close-up must be earned by other close-ups, mediums, or wides**; a lone CU has no scale to vibrate against.
- **The full shot is where bodies do the acting.** If the performance is postural, do not cut to the face.
- **Cutting in** (a tighter angle on the same axis) must keep the subject in roughly the same screen position, or the audience rereads the whole frame.
- **A CU changes the subject of the shot**: once the face fills the frame, the reaction matters more than its cause.

## 2. Angle and height

| Choice | Effect | Use when | Avoid when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eye level | equality, stability, neutrality | you want the viewer to judge rather than submit | the scene needs pressure and nothing else is supplying it |
| Low angle | subject dominates, towers, threatens; space closes in | power, resolve, menace, scale | you also want the audience to feel superior or amused |
| High angle | subject diminishes; the ground and the environment take over | vulnerability, being watched, consequence, defeat | the character is meant to be in control |
| Overhead / top-down | pattern, fate, chessboard | isolation in a system, graphic rhythm | you need eye contact |
| Dutch / tilted | instability, wrongness, alarm | a beat that has gone wrong | a whole sequence (it flattens into style) |
| Ground level | the floor becomes a character; objects loom | animals, children, threat from below, tactile detail | interiors with nothing on the floor to say |

Height is contextual, not arithmetic: low angles make a character dominant only when the surrounding space allows dominance. Katz's *Citizen Kane* example is the caution — the same low angle makes young Kane tower over his guardian and old Kane look small inside Xanadu, because the space around him changed.

## 3. Lens and depth

| Lens | Behaviour | Emotional use |
|---|---|---|
| Wide (≈18–28 mm) | expands space, exaggerates near/far, distorts faces at close range, deep background visible | dynamism, vertigo, aggression, "inside the action", environment as pressure |
| Normal (≈35–50 mm) | close to human vision | neutrality, observation, dialogue |
| Long (≈85–200 mm) | compresses planes, flattens depth, isolates subject, background becomes a wall of texture | surveillance, intimacy at a distance, fatalism, crowding, "the world presses in" |
| Macro | one detail fills the frame | evidence, touch, obsession |

Practical consequences:

- **Wide + close = aggression.** Face distortion and looming foreground.
- **Long + crowd = compression.** Background and foreground collapse together; useful for "trapped in a crowd", fatal for spatial clarity.
- **Long + lateral move = strong graphic travel**, because the compressed background slides past like a cyclorama.
- **Deep focus** puts foreground and background in one argument (a clock in front, a dying man behind). With AI models, deep focus is unreliable: design depth with scale, overlap and light instead of relying on aperture.

## 4. Depth and staging

- **Dramatic circle of action**: the space the action actually occupies. A camera *inside* the circle identifies, pans, and changes subject size; a camera *outside* observes, holds a stable frame, and stays neutral.
- **Foreground / middleground / background** are three arguments, not three distances. Put a meaning in each layer only when it earns its place.
- **Staging in depth** substitutes scale for cutting: near player strong, far player weak — until light, focus, or action reverses it.
- **Separation vs. intersection**: deep space can isolate two people at opposite ends of a room (Welles) or let them pass through each other's space (Renoir). Choose which one the scene is about.
- **Foreground occlusion** (a shoulder, a door frame, a plant) hides and implies: the viewer becomes a concealed witness.

## 5. Camera movement

| Move | What it does | Motivation it needs |
|---|---|---|
| Pan / tilt | reveals, connects, follows | something worth discovering at the end of the move |
| Dolly / push in | increases pressure, narrows the world | a realization or an approach |
| Pull out | reveals context, abandons the subject | new information entering the frame |
| Truck / lateral track | travels with the subject, shows the world passing | subject motion, or a graphic slide |
| Crane / boom | changes the level of the world; scale reveal | a vertical relation worth stating |
| Handheld | presence, instability, breath | the operator's body as a character |
| Orbit / arc | circles a subject, changes background, refuses a fixed judgment | a subject worth orbiting, and time |
| Zoom | changes framing without moving; alienating when used alone | rarely; it reads as a lens decision, not a human one |
| Whip pan | violent connection between two spaces | speed and a target |
| Static | observation, control, patience | nothing — but hold long enough to be a decision |

Rules:

- **One move per shot.** Two moves need a reason the first one ends.
- **Every intermediate frame must compose.** A move is a series of stills, not a path to an endpoint.
- **The move must be motivated by something visible**: a gaze, a gesture, an approach, a vertical shape, a sound. Unmotivated movement reads as drift.
- **Movement changes meaning only if it changes knowledge.** A push-in that reveals nothing is a zoom with ambition.
- **Movement is expensive in AI generation.** Fast or compound moves lose geometry; a slow single move at a readable speed survives best.

## 6. Framing devices

- **Doorway / window**: frames the subject, distances the viewer, turns us into an eavesdropper.
- **Architecture**: columns, corridors, shelves can box, divide, or point at a subject.
- **Mirror**: combines shot and reverse in one frame; splits a character; doubles.
- **Foreground silhouette**: hides the face, making the audience lean in.
- **Open vs. closed framing**: open (cut-off bodies, foreground clutter) feels documentary and involving; closed (clean, balanced, complete) feels staged and distant. Choose the distance you want, then compose to it.

## 7. Transitions

| Transition | Meaning | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Cut | continuous time, new information | the default; must add subject/space/state/viewpoint/time |
| Cut on action | hides the cut inside a movement | stage the action so it overlaps the edit point |
| Match cut | graphic or shape rhyme across time/space | needs a designed matching element |
| Cutaway / bridge | escapes geography, buys time | also a legitimate way to cross the line |
| Clearing the frame | subject exits, empty frame holds 1–2 s | soft, time-passing, very safe |
| Dissolve | time passing, memory, softening | slow; use once |
| Fade | chapter break | once per piece at most |
| Wipe / whip | kinetic, comic, aggressive | style commit |
| Occlusion wipe | a body or object passes the lens | the most physically believable "edit" in AI video |

## 8. AI execution notes

- Shot sizes and moves the models reliably understand: extreme wide / wide / medium / close / extreme close; push in, pull out, pan, truck, follow, orbit, tilt, handheld, static.
- Compound camera language ("dolly in while craning up while racking focus") collapses into mush. Describe the **visible change** instead: "the camera rises until the window drops below the frame edge."
- Lens numbers are weak control; visible consequences are strong. Prefer "the background compresses until the street looks like a wall" over "85 mm".
- Faces at extreme wide are unreadable; performance at extreme close-up is unreadable if the model cannot hold micro-expression. Match the size to what the model can render.
